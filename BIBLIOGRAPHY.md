# Bibliography

The studies and guidelines behind Grindstone's figures, numbered. The code cites them by number, as a book cites its footnotes: `[13]` is the thirteenth entry below. Some are not used at the moment; they stay listed so the numbers never move.

## Body composition

1. Hodgdon J. A., Beckett M. B. (1984). *Prediction of percent body fat for U.S. Navy men from body circumferences and height*. Naval Health Research Center, San Diego, report 84-11. <https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA143890.pdf>
   Used for: A man's fat ratio, read from his waist, neck and height.

2. Hodgdon J. A., Beckett M. B. (1984). *Prediction of percent body fat for U.S. Navy women from body circumferences and height*. Naval Health Research Center, San Diego, report 84-29. <https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/html/tr/ADA146456/index.html>
   Used for: A woman's fat ratio, read from her waist, hip, neck and height.

## Healthy ranges

3. World Health Organization (2000). *Obesity: preventing and managing the global epidemic. Report of a WHO consultation*. WHO Technical Report Series 894. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11234459/>
   Used for: The healthy body mass index, 18.5 to 24.9, behind the recommended mass.

4. Gallagher D., Heymsfield S. B., Heo M., Jebb S. A., Murgatroyd P. R., Sakamoto Y. (2000). *Healthy percentage body fat ranges: an approach for developing guidelines based on body mass index*. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 72(3):694-701. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10966886/>
   Used for: The healthy body fat by age and gender (table 4), behind the recommended fat.

5. Helms E. R., Aragon A. A., Fitschen P. J. (2014). *Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation*. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition 11:20. <https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20>
   Not used at the moment; was: a loss of 0.5 to 1% of the body mass a week, the pace that keeps the muscle.

6. Garthe I., Raastad T., Refsnes P. E., et al. (2011). *Effect of two different weight-loss rates on body composition and strength and power-related performance in elite athletes*. International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism 21(2):97-104. <https://www.sponet.de/sponet/Record/4020931>
   Not used at the moment; was: losing 0.7% a week kept more lean mass than 1.4%, why a fast loss was judged dangerous.

7. Iraki J., Fitschen P., Espinar S., et al. (2019). *Nutrition recommendations for bodybuilders in the off-season: a narrative review*. Sports 7(7):154. <https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154>
   Not used at the moment; was: the band's cap with a gain to aim for, half a percent of the body mass a week, the upper rate of a gain that builds more than it stores.

8. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (2025). *Overweight and obesity management*. NICE guideline NG246. <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246>
   Not used at the moment; was: no more than 0.5 to 1 kg lost a week.

9. Wishnofsky M. (1958). *Caloric equivalent of gained or lost weight*. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 6(5):542-546. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/6.5.542>
   Not used at the moment; was: about 7,700 kcal a kilo of fat tissue, to turn the cap's half a percent of the body mass a week into kcal a day.

## Energy

10. McArdle W. D., Katch F. I., Katch V. L. (2015). *Exercise physiology: nutrition, energy, and human performance (8th edition)*. Wolters Kluwer Health.
   Used for: The basal rate from lean mass (Katch-McArdle).

11. Calcagno M., Kahleova H., Alwarith J., Burgess N. N., Flores R. A., Busta M. L., Barnard N. D. (2019). *The thermic effect of food: a review*. Journal of the American College of Nutrition 38(6):547-551. <https://doi.org/10.1080/07315724.2018.1552544>
   Used for: Digestion priced on the grams of protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre eaten.

12. Levine J. A. (2002). *Non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT)*. Best Practice & Research Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism 16(4):679-702. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12468415/>
   Used for: The day off the clock: sitting, standing, moving and fidgeting.

13. Alpert S. S. (2005). *A limit on the energy transfer rate from the human fat store in hypophagia*. Journal of Theoretical Biology 233(1):1-13. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15615615/>
   Used for: The band's floor, on the fat left to lose above the goal's: what the fat store can spare in a day, 290 kJ (about 69.3 kcal) per kilo.

14. Loucks A. B., Kiens B., Wright H. H. (2011). *Energy availability in athletes*. Journal of Sports Sciences 29(sup1):S7-S15. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21793767/>
   Used for: The band's minimum per kilo of lean mass, plus the burn.

15. Helms E. R., Spence A.-J., Sousa C., et al. (2023). *Effect of small and large energy surpluses on strength, muscle, and skinfold thickness in resistance-trained individuals: a parallel groups design*. Sports Medicine - Open 9:102. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37914977/>
   Not used at the moment; was: the band's cap, maintenance and a twentieth, and its goal tick with a gain to aim for, a small surplus, as a large one mostly adds fat.

## Activity

16. Herrmann S. D., Willis E. A., Ainsworth B. E., et al. (2024). *2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities: a third update of the energy costs of human activities*. Journal of Sport and Health Science 13(1):6-12. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38242596/>
   Used for: The MET of each exercise, burnt above rest.

17. Tudor-Locke C., Craig C. L., Brown W. J., et al. (2011). *How many steps/day are enough? for adults*. International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity 8:79. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3197470/>
   Used for: The usual day's steps: 2500, the most of basal activity, the workouts logged counting the rest.

18. Buckley J. P., Hedge A., Yates T., et al. (2015). *The sedentary office: an expert statement on the growing case for change towards better health and productivity*. British Journal of Sports Medicine 49(21):1357-1362. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26034192/>
   Used for: The usual day's 2 hours standing, the first step towards 4.

19. Ekelund U., Steene-Johannessen J., Brown W. J., et al. (2016). *Does physical activity attenuate, or even eliminate, the detrimental association of sitting time with mortality? A harmonised meta-analysis of data from more than 1 million men and women*. The Lancet 388(10051):1302-1310. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475271/>
   Used for: The usual day's 8 hours sitting at most, past which the least active die sooner.

20. Bull F. C., Al-Ansari S. S., Biddle S., et al. (2020). *World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour*. British Journal of Sports Medicine 54(24):1451-1462. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955>
   Used for: Less sitting and more moving, at any intensity: the direction of the usual day; and the burn target, 150 minutes a week of moderate activity priced on the body weighed.

## Macronutrients

21. Jäger R., Kerksick C. M., Campbell B. I., et al. (2017). *International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise*. Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition 14:20. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/>
   Used for: Protein of 1.4 to 2.0 g per kilo for those who train.

22. Morton R. W., Murphy K. T., McKellar S. R., et al. (2018). *A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults*. British Journal of Sports Medicine 52(6):376-384. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5867436/>
   Used for: Protein of 1.6 g per kilo, where the gains in lean mass from training level off.

23. EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (2010). *Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for fats, including saturated fatty acids, polyunsaturated fatty acids, monounsaturated fatty acids, trans fatty acids, and cholesterol*. EFSA Journal 8(3):1461. <https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1461>
   Used for: Fat of 20 to 35% of the day's energy.

24. World Health Organization (2023). *Total fat intake for the prevention of unhealthy weight gain in adults and children: WHO guideline*. World Health Organization, Geneva. <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240073654>
   Used for: Fat of 30% of the day's energy at most.

25. EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (2010). *Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for carbohydrates and dietary fibre*. EFSA Journal 8(3):1462. <https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1462>
   Used for: Fibre of 25 g a day for adults.

26. World Health Organization (2023). *Carbohydrate intake for adults and children: WHO guideline*. World Health Organization, Geneva. <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240073593>
   Used for: Fibre of at least 25 g a day, behind the recommended fibre.

27. Institute of Medicine (2005). *Dietary Reference Intakes for energy, carbohydrate, fiber, fat, fatty acids, cholesterol, protein, and amino acids*. The National Academies Press, Washington. <https://doi.org/10.17226/10490>
   Used for: At least 130 g of carbohydrates, and fibre of 14 g per 1000 kcal.

## Water

28. EFSA Panel on Dietetic Products, Nutrition and Allergies (2010). *Scientific Opinion on Dietary Reference Values for water*. EFSA Journal 8(3):1459. <https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1459>
   Used for: Before a lean mass is known, water of 2.5 L a day for men and 2.0 L for women, four fifths of it from drinks.

29. Yamada Y., Zhang X., Henderson M. E. T., et al. (2022). *Variation in human water turnover associated with environmental and lifestyle factors*. Science 378(6622):909-915. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm8668>
   Used for: The day's target: the water turned over a day for a lean mass and an age, at the paper's reference climate, 85% of it taken in, four fifths of that drunk.

30. Westerterp K. R., Plasqui G., Goris A. H. C. (2005). *Water loss as a function of energy intake, physical activity and season*. British Journal of Nutrition 93(2):199-203. <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/water-loss-as-a-function-of-energy-intake-physical-activity-and-season/51D08EEA77F1019DD393667429046510>
   Used for: The floor of a millilitre per kcal spent, and 1.25 mL per kcal burnt in the target.

31. Joo M. A., Kim E. Y. (2013). *Hyponatremia caused by excessive intake of water as a form of child abuse*. Annals of Pediatric Endocrinology & Metabolism 18(2):95-98. <https://doi.org/10.6065/apem.2013.18.2.95>
   Used for: Kidneys clearing no more than 0.8 to 1 L of water an hour, the reason to spread the cups out.
